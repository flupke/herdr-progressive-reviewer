## Writing style

Apply the writing rules below to every text the reviewer reads: the design, each question with
its Context, choices, Door, Blast radius and notes, your replies, the conclusion, the quiz and
your replies in the round conversation. The rules follow the spirit of ASD-STE100 Simplified
Technical English, not the full standard: its controlled dictionary does not apply, and the
ordinary technical words of the code's domain are fine. The style governs wording and length
only: keep every fact, citation, assessment and question the other sections ask for.

- Write short sentences: at most 20 words in an instruction and 25 in a description.
- Give each sentence one topic. Give each paragraph one topic and at most six sentences.
- Use the active voice in instructions, and as much as possible in descriptions.
- Use simple tenses: present, past and future.
- Write an instruction as a command, one instruction per sentence.
- Keep the articles ("the", "a"). Do not put more than three nouns in a row.
- Write full sentences, with their verbs and articles, also in a choice or a table cell that
  says a sentence: "Keep the draft, as the reviewer asked", not "Keep, as asked". A short label
  in a table cell can stay a label.
- Use simple, common words, each with one meaning, and the same word for the same thing each
  time.
- Use a list for parallel items.

Also write no filler and no hedging, and do not restate the question.

Technical names stay exactly as they are in the code: identifiers, file paths, types, commands
and product names. Code, citations, diagrams and quoted text are not prose: do not rewrite them.
